Arms & Artifacts ​

Every hero fights with what they carry: two hand slots and three accessory slots, filled from what the world gives up. An item's stats add straight onto the wearer's own, and speed does double duty: it feeds both battle turns and how far the party marches in a day.

How the slots work ​

TypeHandsNotes
Blade1Sword, axe, or mace; pairs well with a shield
Dagger1Light steel; rogues make pairs of them sing
Greatblade2Heavy iron; warriors break the rules with these
Bow2Shooters' reach on the tactical field
Staff2The caster's arm
Shield1Guard for the off hand (or the main)
Accessory0Rings, charms, and lenses; three may be worn

Two tricks of the trade change everything about who wants what:

  • Warriors are strong enough to wield a two-handed weapon in each hand. Two greatblades on one veteran is the heaviest hitting in the game.
  • Rogues shoot bows half again as hard (+50% of the bow's ranged power), and a matched pair of daggers turns them into a whirlwind: +3 melee and +1 speed on top of the steel itself.

Where artifacts come from ​

  • The fallen. The victor of any battle strips the dead of their gear. What your enemies carry, you can inherit.
  • Hoards. Marauder-held buildings, and every hold the blight has swallowed, have a 40% chance of yielding an artifact when taken. A hero with Plunder raises that to 60%.
  • Dungeon chests. Storming a garrisoned building on the tactical field ends at the bottom floor with a prize: coin and stores, an artifact, or on rare occasion a caged hero.

Geography is rarity. Treasure runs on the same menace scale as garrison muscle: the guild ring and outer wilds yield tier-1 arms, the blight's edge tier 2, and the deep blight alone gives up the tier-3 relics. Richer finds always mean harder fights.

Logistics matter. New finds ride in the finding party's satchel and can be equipped by that party anywhere. The guild vault opens only inside your own cities: walk the satchel in and Store all in vault (or hold one item's chip to stow just it), or send a scout to courier it home so another hero can draw from it. Unequipping in the field keeps the piece on the party's backs.

Kitting a hero ​

  • A warrior Lord wants two greatblades and armor accessories: the Entropy Reaper pair (⚔+14) with a Crucible Girdle covers the lost guard.
  • A rogue wants the best bow in the world plus paired daggers held in reserve, with speed accessories to strike first and slip away.
  • An alchemist stays alive by reach: a defensive staff, then the Winged Philter or Mirror-adjacent guard so the melee never arrives.
  • Remember the party marches at its slowest member's speed: one 👟−1 item on the wrong unit slows the whole column.
