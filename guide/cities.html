Cities & Holdings ​

Tap one of your cities and the city board opens straight away — with or without a party selected on it. Cities level from 1 to 3 — each level adds job slots, a floor to the skyline, and eventually unlocks the best projects.

Jobs ​

Spare Followers work the city's crafts: the Market (gold), Tavern (elixir), Temple (reagents), the Farms (food), the Magistrate (infamy) and — once you have raised one — the Bestiary (beast lore).

Every craft pays the same daily worth; only the coin differs. One keeper earns 25 gold, or 16 elixir, or 12 reagents, or 25 food — the same value at the rates guilds trade them. So posting a keeper is a choice of what your city makes, never of how much. The Magistrate is the exception: infamy is not traded, and it earns at its own slow pace. Infamy still matters — your deeds plus your magistrates decide how many new Followers arrive every seventh day.

A posting is permanent

A Follower settles into their craft and does not come back out. Spend them like skill points: the number posted to a craft is that craft's level, and some things — beast lore especially — will not open until a craft has been raised high enough.

Walls and garrisons ​

Any holding takes at most 10 soldiers — enough to hold a gate, not enough to make a fortress unassailable.

Defenders fight a little steadier for standing behind stone: a city or Grand Athanor is worth something on its own, and Walls raised in the city ring are worth more again, growing with their level. They also sharpen the drill of everyone posted behind them — in peacetime and during a defence.

Food & farms ​

The faithful march on bread, not coin: every recruit costs 1 Follower, 40 food and 20 elixir — gold buys mercenaries, structures and training, never the faithful. Cities grow a base crop (villages a smaller one), Farm workers grow more, and for real granaries you settle farmsteads: hire a Farm Hand at any of your cities (100🪙), march them out with a party, and on held, empty ground the command bar's 🌾 slot lights up to raise the farmstead. The hand settles there for good; the farmstead yields 10 food a day at full control — and like any holding of yours, its fences ward the blight. A ring of farms is both a food supply and a wall.

Training ​

Every unit standing at the building — in a party or the garrison — can buy its next level for gold and elixir. The drillmaster only charges for what is left to learn: a soldier already three-quarters of the way to the next level pays a quarter of the list price, so battle scars are money in the bank. Training takes the day; the queue shows who's drilling. Keep a high-level veteran garrisoned and new recruits start at half their level.

Barracks train warriors, Laboratories train alchemists, Shadow Guilds train rogues; cities and Grand Athanors drill warriors. Tap any owned trainer building on the map to open its hall directly.

Build ​

Cities can raise new structures on free land beside them — a Barracks, Laboratory or Distillery, and at city level 3 a Shadow Guild. One project at a time; a few days each — and one of each per city. Once a structure stands, its row becomes the upgrade path instead: a structure can rise up to its city's level (raise the city first to raise its halls higher). Each level multiplies the structure's daily yield, and each level above the first cuts training costs at that hall by 15%. Tap the structure on the map to see exactly what an upgrade buys and costs.

Research ​

The Town Hall researches hero skills, one project at a time, guild-wide once learned. Nothing is granted: even a Lord's own class art (Rally, Ambush, Transmute Blast) is a study — the cheapest on the bench, and the one to start with. A level‑2 city unlocks the deeper studies (Shield Wall, Plunder, Field Mending) and a level‑3 city the masterworks (Warcry, Overwhelm, Quicksilver March). Once researched, assign the skill on any matching hero's sheet.

The Town Hall is not the only school. A Barracks, Laboratory, Shadow Guild or Bestiary teaches arts of its own trade that the hall never offers — which is a reason to raise them past drilling in them. The full tree lives in Skills & Research.

Defense ​

If a visible enemy prowls within sight of an undefended holding, the game warns you and offers an emergency muster — recruits or mercenaries straight onto the walls. The locate button's jump list shows every city, flagging any whose walls stand empty.

Garrison duty pays on its own, too: a soldier standing a wall earns a slow trickle of experience every day — the wall is a drill yard. Heroes learn from deeds, not drills, but a green recruit posted to a quiet hold comes back sharper. Between the drills and their wages, a standing garrison keeps your surplus working instead of piling up.
