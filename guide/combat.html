Battles & Dungeons ​

Joining battle ​

March onto an enemy and the game pauses to ask how you want to fight: Trust the captains for a quick outcome, or ⚔ Take the field to fight the battle yourself. You can also Fall back, unless you have been ambushed.

Before you choose, your captains size up the fight and say so plainly, from "expect a rout" down to "counsel against this fight". When the counsel runs red, believe them.

Every engagement begins one of three ways. Ambushes either way are rare (a few percent, swayed by level difference — and rogue lords with the Ambush skill spring them more often):

  • Sprung ambush — you catch them unaware. On the field they stand asleep and take no turns until first struck.
  • Battle — the sides face off and act in turns.
  • Ambushed! — the enemy surrounds your party mid-field, and there is no declining the fight.

The tactical field ​

Turns run by unit speed; each unit gets one move and one action. Blue tiles show where you can step. Tap one to preview the move (and the red reach it would give you), then confirm. Red tiles show your weapon's reach: adjacent for melee, three tiles for shooters. Heroes with Transmute Blast get a cast mode that burns half their Essence on one target. Rally, Shield Wall and Overwhelm all apply on the field, and Withdraw breaks off the fight. Pinch to zoom into the melee and drag to pan while zoomed, or use the + / − / ⤢ pad in the corner.

High ground and hard landings ​

No field is flat. The ground rises in half-step ramps toward hills and crags, and boulders squat on the low ground where nothing can walk. Use them:

  • Shooters reach one tile further for every full height they hold above their target. A bowman on a crag commands the valley.
  • Half-step ramps climb free. A full-height jump up costs an extra move point, and nothing on foot climbs more than one full height in a step.
  • You can leap down any distance, but a plunge past three heights deals fall damage on the landing (the move preview warns you first). A fall bruises; it never kills outright.
  • Winged creatures such as the Chimera ignore all of it: heights, ramps, falls.
  • Boulders block the charge. Put one between your archer and a foe's sword and make them walk the long way around.

Dungeon floors ​

Garrisoned buildings fight floor by floor — the defenders split across the building's floors, and the STAIR DOWN hatch is visible on the far side of every hall. Clear the floor and the stair lights up: descend and fight on. With a rogue hero you have two more options:

  • Sneak past the next floor at the door — 25–75% by level. Fail, and that floor falls on you as an ambush.
  • Flee down mid-fight — the whole party runs for the stair, eating parting blows based on how close the enemies stand and how fast each runner is. Slow units can die on the stairs.

The prize at the bottom ​

Every stormed hold ends in a prize: a chest of coin and stores scaled by how deep you fought, an artifact — finer the closer to the world's blighted heart — or, rarely, a caged hero who joins their rescuer. Victors also strip the fallen of their gear, and beaten marauder garrisons often guarded a hoard.
