Heroes & Parties ​

The hero panel ​

Selecting a party shows its hero in the lower-left: vitals, party size, movement, travel status, and action buttons. The ✕ deselects; Units expands a bar of every member of the party:

Tap any card (or the hero's portrait) to open their sheet.

The hero sheet ​

Every hero has two hand slots and three accessory slots. Weapons, shields and artifacts come from battle spoils, marauder hoards, and dungeon chests, and ride in the satchel of the party that found them. Tap an item there to equip it. The guild vault is another matter: it opens only inside one of your cities, so what one party finds in the far wilds reaches another party's hands by walking it home, by hand or by scout. A few tricks of the trade:

  • Warriors are strong enough to wield a two-handed weapon in each hand.
  • Rogues shoot bows half again as hard, and a matched pair of daggers turns them into a whirlwind.

Heroes also carry up to three skills. The class skill is innate; the rest are researched at a city's Town Hall and can then be assigned to any hero of the right class.

Growing your forces ​

Units level up from battle (experience for wounds dealt and kills) or paid training at the right building. Recruiting takes a Follower plus gold and elixir; mercenaries cost pure gold but demand pay every day and walk the moment you miss it. A veteran of the same class standing at the recruiting building mentors newcomers — recruits start at half the mentor's level.

Champions and more heroes ​

A veteran of level 5+, standing in one of your cities, can be promoted to Champion — a hero who can lead a party of their own (every hero leads up to 8 units). Split champions off from the Muster board to run two armies at once. Rarely, a dungeon holds a caged hero who will join their rescuer — champion-ranked, but expensive to keep.

The Muster board and reinforcements ​

At any of your buildings, Muster moves units between parties, the building's garrison, and other parties on the same tile — and it's where promotions happen.

Reinforce musters a recruit at any of your trainer holdings and marches them cross-country to a hero — but the receiving party holds position until every marcher arrives. The 8-slot cap counts marchers on the road, contested holdings (under half their land held) cannot muster at all, and you can recall a marcher home to move early.

Scouts ​

Any of your cities will hire a Scout into the party standing there: fast, frail, and worth every coin. Send one out from the hero panel and choose their errand:

  • Chart the unknown: the scout rides for unexplored country, burning off the fog of war in a wide circle as they go, then turns for home.
  • Carry the finds home: the scout loads up everything in the party's satchel and delivers it to the vault of your nearest city.

Either way the party keeps marching; it never waits on a scout. The return leg chases wherever the party has gone since, so a three-day errand becomes a longer one if you have marched two days the other way. A scout in the party also slows it no more than any rogue, but mind that they are poor fighters when the line forms.
